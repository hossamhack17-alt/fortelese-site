A "free" current account is free on the condition that you never fall below a balance, never use certain ATMs, and never pay in cash abroad. Most people break one of these conditions once, and then again next month.

This is a checklist of the charges that rarely make it into the marketing, plus a method to audit your own account.

## 1. The charges that hide

| Charge | Where it appears |
|---|---|
| Monthly maintenance fee | Only if you drop below the minimum balance |
| Arranged overdraft interest | Daily, so the monthly figure looks small |
| Unarranged overdraft fee | A flat charge plus daily interest |
| ATM fee abroad | Per withdrawal, often with a poor exchange rate |
| Foreign transaction fee | A percentage on every card purchase |
| Paper statement fee | Monthly, if you have not gone digital |
| Card replacement | Flat fee, faster if urgent |

## 2. How to audit your account in fifteen minutes

1. Download twelve months of statements as a spreadsheet or CSV.
2. Filter for the words fee, charge, interest, and any recurring debit you do not recognise.
3. Sum by type. Most people find one category dominates.
4. Compare that total against the account's advertised benefits.
5. Check whether a different account type at the same bank removes the biggest item.

## 3. Overdraft mechanics worth understanding

An arranged overdraft is borrowing. Interest usually accrues daily on the amount and the number of days, so a small balance held for three weeks can cost more than a larger balance held for two days.

The practical rule: treat any overdraft balance as a loan with a deadline, not as a buffer.

## 4. Fees abroad

Two charges often stack: a withdrawal fee and a currency conversion markup. The cheapest pattern is usually a card designed for travel with no foreign transaction fee, funded by a transfer rather than a cash withdrawal.

## 5. Questions to ask your bank

1. What is the minimum balance to avoid the monthly fee?
2. What is the daily interest rate on an arranged overdraft?
3. Is there a fee for using an ATM abroad, and what markup applies?
4. Does a paper statement cost extra?
5. Can I switch account type without a credit check?

## 6. A simple annual review

Once a year, total the fees you paid, then compare against one alternative account. Switching is usually straightforward, but check for direct debits and incoming salary before you move.

## 7. Mistakes that cost money

- Paying for a packaged account whose benefits you never claim.
- Using a debit card abroad without checking the markup.
- Keeping a low balance to "save" and paying a monthly fee instead.
- Ignoring a small overdraft that recurs monthly.

## Disclaimer

This article is general education, not financial advice. Rates, fees and eligibility change and differ by provider; verify current terms in writing before you commit.
