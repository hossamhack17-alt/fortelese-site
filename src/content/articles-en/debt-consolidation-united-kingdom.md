Consolidation turns several payments into one. That is an administrative improvement, not a financial one. Whether it helps depends entirely on the total cost and on what happens to the habits that created the debt.

## 1. What consolidation actually changes

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| One payment instead of several | Easier to manage, same underlying debt |
| A possibly lower monthly payment | Usually achieved by extending the term |
| A possibly lower interest rate | Helpful if the new rate truly is lower after fees |
| A longer term | Higher total interest unless the rate falls sharply |

A lower payment with a longer term is not a saving; it is a deferral.

## 2. When it can genuinely help

- The new rate is materially lower after all fees.
- You can afford the new payment without relying on credit again.
- You have a written plan to close the accounts you consolidated.
- The term is not stretched just to fit a payment you cannot really afford.

## 3. When it usually fails

- The old cards stay open and get used again.
- The payment fits only in a good month.
- Fees are rolled into the new balance without being totalled.
- The product is secured against your home, converting unsecured debt into a risk to your property.

## 4. The comparison that matters

Write two columns: current situation and proposed situation.

| Item | Current | Proposed |
|---|---|---|
| Total balance | | |
| Interest rate after fees | | |
| Months remaining | | |
| Total interest to be paid | | |
| Monthly payment | | |
| Is it secured? | | |

If the total interest paid does not fall, the consolidation is cosmetic.

## 5. Alternatives to consider first

- A payment plan agreed directly with each lender.
- Free debt advice services that assess affordability before any product is sold.
- A balance transfer with a genuine promotional period, if you can clear the balance within it.
- Reducing outgoings and applying the difference to the highest-rate debt.

## 6. Questions to ask before signing

1. What is the total amount repayable, including fees?
2. Is the loan secured against my home?
3. What is the early repayment charge?
4. What happens if I miss a payment?
5. Will closing the old accounts affect my credit file, and how?
6. What is the exit route if my income changes?

## 7. The habit test

Consolidation works when the monthly surplus that was spread across several payments now goes to one payment and nothing new is borrowed for at least six months. If a new balance appears on a card within that window, the problem is the budget, not the number of payments.
