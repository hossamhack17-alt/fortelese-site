A cash-back card is a discount on spending you were going to do anyway, and an expensive loan on spending you were not. The only number that matters is the annual net return: rewards earned, minus fees, minus any interest paid.

## 1. The formula

```
Net annual value = rewards earned − annual fee − interest paid − missed rewards
```

Where "missed rewards" is the amount you gave up by not routing spending through the higher-rate category, or by leaving rewards unredeemed.

A card advertising a high headline rate can still be worth less than a simpler card, if it charges an annual fee your spending does not recover.

## 2. Work out your break-even spending

If a card has an annual fee and pays a flat rate, the break-even spend is:

```
Break-even spend = annual fee ÷ reward rate
```

At one and a half percent, a ninety-five dollar fee needs roughly six thousand three hundred dollars of annual spending just to break even. Below that, the free card wins.

## 3. Category caps change everything

Rotating or tiered cards often apply a high rate to a capped amount, then drop to a low rate. Model two numbers:

- How much of your spending actually falls inside the elevated category.
- How much falls outside it, at the base rate.

Most people overestimate the first and ignore the second.

## 4. Interest destroys the benefit

At a typical credit-card interest rate, carrying a balance for a single month can exceed a full year of rewards on the same amount. If you sometimes carry a balance, the mathematically correct move is a no-fee card and a payoff plan, not a rewards card.

## 5. Redemption rules to read

- Minimum redemption thresholds.
- Whether rewards expire.
- Whether statement credit, deposit or travel redemption gives different value.
- Whether rewards are forfeited if the account is closed.

A reward you cannot conveniently redeem is not a reward.

## 7. Habits that keep the value positive

- Pay the statement balance in full, every month.
- Keep total utilisation low.
- Review the category calendar at the start of each quarter.
- Redeem on a schedule rather than accumulating indefinitely.
- Re-evaluate annually against a free alternative.
