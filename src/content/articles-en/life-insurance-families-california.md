Most people either buy too little life cover or buy a round number that was never calculated. The right amount is not a rule of thumb; it is the sum of the obligations your family would face if your income stopped tomorrow.

## 1. Start from obligations, not from a multiple

Ignoring sales shorthand such as "ten times your salary", build the number:

| Component | How to estimate |
|---|---|
| Mortgage or rent | Outstanding balance, or years of rent you want covered |
| Other debts | Car loans, student loans, credit cards |
| Income replacement | Years of support your family needs, at a realistic annual figure |
| Childcare and education | Current costs projected forward |
| Final expenses | A modest, realistic figure |
| Emergency buffer | Several months of household costs |

Add these, then subtract existing cover and liquid savings. The remainder is the gap.

## 2. Term length: match the obligation

Cover should last until the obligation ends, not longer:

- Mortgage cover: align with the remaining loan term.
- Child-raising cover: until the youngest child is financially independent.
- Income replacement: the number of years your household would need support.

A long term on a small need costs more than necessary; a short term on a long obligation leaves a gap in the years it matters most.

## 3. Term versus permanent

| Type | Best suited to |
|---|---|
| Term | Pure protection for a defined period, usually the lowest cost per unit of cover |
| Whole life | Lifelong cover with a savings element, higher cost |
| Return of premium | Higher premiums for a refund feature many people never use |

For most families with a mortgage and young children, term cover does the job at a fraction of the cost.

## 4. Questions to ask before buying

1. Is the premium guaranteed or can it rise?
2. What happens if I stop paying?
3. Are there medical questions, and what happens to my answers?
4. Does the policy pay for terminal illness before death?
5. Is the beneficiary designation correct and up to date?
6. What is the conversion option if my health changes?

## 5. Where claims go wrong

- Beneficiary details left as an old spouse or a former address.
- Material information omitted at application.
- Cover lapsing during a payment gap.
- Assuming employer group cover is enough and portable; it usually is not.

## 6. A simple annual review

Once a year, check three numbers: outstanding mortgage, total debts, and the amount your household would need annually. If any has changed materially, revisit the cover.

## Disclaimer

This article is general education, not financial, insurance or legal advice. Cover limits, exclusions and fees differ by policy and by provider; rely on the wording in the document you actually sign and on the relevant regulator.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this a recommendation?** No. It explains what to check; it names no provider to buy from.

**Are the limits the same everywhere?** No. They are set by each policy and each market's rules.

**Where do I verify the terms?** In the policy schedule and the full policy wording, plus the official regulator.
