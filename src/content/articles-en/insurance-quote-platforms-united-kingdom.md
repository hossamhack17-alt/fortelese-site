A comparison site gives you a price in ninety seconds. The question is whether that price still exists when you claim. Most disputes in UK insurance are not about whether cover existed, but about whether the details you entered matched what the insurer later investigated.

## 1. What comparison platforms actually do

They collect your answers, pass them to insurers or brokers, and show the resulting quotes. They are not the insurer, and your contract is with the insurer. That matters for two reasons:

- The platform's default answers may not match your situation.
- The cheapest quote often comes from the strictest set of assumptions.

## 2. Where accuracy breaks down

Typical gaps between what the user enters and what the insurer later checks:

| Field | Common shortcut | What insurers verify |
|---|---|---|
| Occupation | Picking a broad category | Job title and duties |
| Mileage | A round estimate | MOT records and claims history |
| Modifications | "None" by default | Vehicle specification |
| Claims history | Forgetting a small claim | Shared claims databases |
| Address details | Postcode only | Parking arrangements, occupancy |

Any mismatch can be treated as misrepresentation, which can affect a claim even when the difference looks trivial.

## 3. The details that move your premium most

- Claims history in recent years.
- Vehicle or property specifics such as age, construction and security.
- Where it is kept overnight.
- Voluntary excess: a higher excess lowers the premium but raises your exposure.
- Payment method: monthly instalments usually cost more than annual payment.

## 4. How to compare properly

1. Decide your required cover level first, not your budget.
2. Enter identical details on two platforms to see the spread.
3. Read the policy summary for exclusions, not just the price.
4. Check the excess before comparing final numbers.
5. Quote directly with one or two insurers to see the difference.
6. Screenshot the details you entered; you will need them if a claim is queried.

## 5. Where cheap goes wrong

- Excess set so high that a small claim is not worth making.
- Exclusions for the very thing you are insuring against.
- Assumptions about drivers or occupants that do not match reality.
- Monthly payment fees that erase the headline saving.

## 6. Buying versus renewing

Renewal quotes are frequently higher than new-customer quotes for identical cover. It is worth checking the market each year, but do not cancel until the new policy is confirmed active, because a gap in cover can be costly.

## 7. Practical checklist before you buy

1. Cover level decided.
2. Details verified against documents.
3. Excess understood.
4. Exclusions read.
5. Total annual cost compared, not monthly.
6. Policy documents saved.
