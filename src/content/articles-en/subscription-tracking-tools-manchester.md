Nobody decides to spend a hundred pounds a month on subscriptions. It accumulates one small card payment at a time, and by the time you notice, it has become background noise. The fix is mechanical: audit, cancel, and set a review that costs ten minutes a month.

## 1. The three-month audit

Pull three months of bank and card statements. Search for repeat debits with the same amount or merchant. Categories usually missed:

- App and cloud storage upgrades.
- Streaming services bundled with a device.
- Gym or club memberships you no longer use.
- Software trials that converted automatically.
- Delivery memberships.
- Child activity apps paid annually.

Write each one as a row: name, amount, frequency, next renewal date, cancellation route.

## 2. Trials versus rolling contracts

| Type | Risk | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Free trial converting automatically | Highest | Put the end date in a calendar on the day you sign up |
| Monthly rolling | Medium | Cancel at least two days before renewal |
| Annual paid upfront | Lowest frequency, highest single loss | Track the renewal date; decide a month before |
| Bundled with hardware | Easy to miss | Review with the hardware contract annually |

## 3. Cancellation that actually works

1. Cancel in the account settings, not inside the app alone.
2. Save the confirmation email or take a screenshot showing the end date.
3. Check the next statement; if the charge appears, contact the provider with the proof.
4. If unresolved, ask your card provider about a dispute within their time limits.

Deleting an app does not cancel a subscription; the payment continues.

## 4. Set a ceiling and a review

Choose a monthly ceiling you will actually respect. Then set one recurring appointment, first of each month, to:

- Compare this month's subscription total against the ceiling.
- Cancel anything unused for thirty days.
- Note which annual renewals are coming in the next three months.

## 5. Family sharing versus separate plans

Before cutting, check whether a household plan costs less than the sum of individual accounts. Conversely, a household plan paying for people who never use it is the most expensive option of all.

## 6. Where the money should go

Direct the difference to the emergency fund or the highest-rate debt, automatically. If it stays in the spending account, it will be absorbed within two months and the audit will feel pointless.

## 7. Mistakes that keep the leak open

- Relying on memory rather than statements.
- Buying a subscription tracker and never using it.
- Not cancelling before the renewal date.
- Ignoring small annual charges because they are infrequent.
- Believing a "free" tier cannot convert to paid.
