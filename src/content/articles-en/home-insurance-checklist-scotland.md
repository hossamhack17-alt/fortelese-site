Two policies with the same headline price can behave very differently the first time you claim. The difference is almost never the premium; it is the limits buried in the schedule and the exclusions written in the wording.

This checklist walks through the items that most often surprise Scottish homeowners, without naming any insurer.

## 1. Buildings versus contents

- **Buildings** covers the structure: walls, roof, fixtures, fitted kitchens and bathrooms.
- **Contents** covers what you would take with you if you moved: furniture, electronics, clothing.

Flats and tenements blur the line: the factor or the building policy may cover parts you assume are yours. Ask in writing which parts are covered by which policy before a leak forces the question.

## 2. Limits that quietly bite

| Item | Typical trap |
|---|---|
| Single article limit | A laptop or bike worth more than the per-item cap |
| Total valuables limit | Jewellery and watches under one combined cap |
| Outbuildings | Sheds, garages and garden equipment often capped low |
| Bicycles | Frequently excluded or capped when away from home |
| Temporary accommodation | A time or cost cap after a major event |
| Accidental damage | A separate add-on, not always included |

Check the schedule wording; a higher headline sum insured does not automatically raise these sub-limits.

## 3. Exclusions that follow the weather

Scotland's climate produces predictable claim types, and policies respond to them differently:

- **Storm and flood**: check the definition of storm, and whether flood cover is included.
- **Escape of water**: the single most common domestic claim. Check whether tracing and access costs are covered.
- **Frost damage**: often conditional on the property being heated or drained while empty.
- **Subsidence**: usually excluded or available only via a separate policy.

## 4. Conditions that can void a claim

- Leaving the property unoccupied beyond the stated number of days without notifying the insurer.
- Not maintaining the roof or gutters when damage was visible.
- Failing to disclose a prior flood or subsidence claim.
- Running a business from home without telling the insurer.

## 5. Questions to ask at renewal

1. What is the single-article contents limit?
2. Is accidental damage included or an add-on?
3. Is flood cover included, and what is the definition of storm?
4. What is the unoccupied period allowed?
5. Are outbuildings and bikes covered away from home?
6. What is the excess for water damage specifically?

## 6. A practical way to value your contents

Walk through each room and write down replacement cost, not what you paid. Use current retail prices for equivalents. Most people underestimate by a wide margin, and average clauses can reduce a payout if you are under-insured.

## 7. Mistakes that cost money at claim time

- Insuring for the mortgage balance instead of rebuild cost.
- Forgetting the home office equipment.
- Assuming a new-for-old replacement without checking age limits.
- Never reviewing the sum insured after a renovation.
- Not photographing rooms and receipts.

## Disclaimer

This article is general education, not financial, insurance or legal advice. Cover limits, exclusions and fees differ by policy and by provider; rely on the wording in the document you actually sign and on the relevant regulator.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this a recommendation?** No. It explains what to check; it names no provider to buy from.

**Are the limits the same everywhere?** No. They are set by each policy and each market's rules.

**Where do I verify the terms?** In the policy schedule and the full policy wording, plus the official regulator.
