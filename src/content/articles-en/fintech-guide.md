Financial apps are easy to install and hard to evaluate. The interface looks clean, onboarding takes four minutes, and the real questions only surface when something goes wrong: where is my money actually held, who can I complain to, and what happens to my balance if the company shuts down?

This guide gives you a checklist to answer those questions in about fifteen minutes.

## 1. Who holds the licence?

An app is a front end. Behind it there is usually a licensed bank or payment institution that holds customer funds. Find the answers to two questions:

- Which regulated entity holds my money?
- Is that entity licensed in my country, and is the licence visible on the regulator's own website?

If the marketing says "banking" but the licence belongs to an entity in another jurisdiction, your protections may be different from what you assume.

## 2. Where does the money sit?

Three common models, with very different risk:

| Model | Where funds sit | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Full bank account | In your name at a licensed bank | Deposit protection applies |
| E-money or wallet | Pooled with other customers at a bank | Protection depends on local rules |
| Custody or brokerage | Held by a custodian, often invested | Market risk; protection is different |

Knowing which model you are using tells you what is protected and what is exposed.

## 3. The fee that never appears as a fee

For multi-currency apps, the real cost is usually the exchange-rate markup, not the transfer fee. Compare the rate you are quoted against a mid-market reference rate at the same moment. A "zero fee" transfer can cost more than a visible one percent fee.

Ask for the total amount your recipient receives, then compare that number across providers. That is the only honest comparison.

## 4. What happens if the app closes?

Check the terms for:

- The process if the service is discontinued.
- How fast funds are returned, and to where.
- Whether your transaction history is exportable.
- Whether there is a complaints procedure with a named authority.

A provider that cannot answer these plainly in writing is a provider you should not keep a large balance with.

## 5. Security questions worth asking

- Is two-factor authentication available and enabled by default?
- Can I lock the card and freeze the account from the app?
- Are login notifications sent for new devices?
- Is there a waiting period after changing the phone number or email?

Most account takeovers start with a weak recovery channel, not a sophisticated hack.

## 6. A 15-minute evaluation checklist

1. Licence holder identified and verified on the regulator's site.
2. Money-holding model understood.
3. Total recipient amount compared across two providers.
4. Shutdown and complaints process read.
5. Two-factor enabled, recovery channel hardened.
6. Transaction history export tested once.
7. A daily-use balance limit set.

## 7. Mistakes that cost people money

- Treating an app balance as a savings account.
- Comparing transfer fees while ignoring the exchange rate.
- Skipping the terms and assuming deposit protection.
- Reusing one email and phone number across every financial account.
- Keeping a large balance in a wallet with no clear protection scheme.

## 8. A sensible way to use fintech

Use apps where they are strongest: small transfers, day-to-day spending, and multi-currency conversion with transparent pricing. Keep savings and large balances in a licensed institution whose protections you can verify in writing.

## Disclaimer

This article is general education, not financial, insurance or legal advice. Terms change and differ by provider; rely on the document you actually sign and on the relevant regulator.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this a recommendation?** No. It explains how to read and compare offers; it names no provider to buy from.

**Will the numbers be the same for me?** No. Amounts, fees and eligibility depend on your profile and the provider's current terms.

**Where do I verify the rules?** In the signed document and with the official regulator for that market.
