Manchester's housing stock mixes Victorian terraces, interwar semis and newer apartments, and each brings a different set of predictable defects. A survey that lists them plainly is worth more than a cheaper report that says "no significant issues" without detail.

## 1. Damp: the costliest recurring finding

Look for three distinct causes, because the remedy differs:

| Cause | Typical sign | Remedy scope |
|---|---|---|
| Rising damp | Tide mark low on the wall | Injection or membrane, disruptive |
| Penetrating damp | Localised patch after rain | Roof, gutter or pointing repair |
| Condensation | Mould in corners, window condensation | Ventilation and heating behaviour |

Surveys often label all three as "damp". Ask specifically which type was found and what evidence supports it, because the remedies are not interchangeable.

## 2. Roof and rainwater goods

- Approximate age of the covering and whether any part has been replaced.
- Condition of gutters and downpipes; blocked gutters cause wall and foundation damage.
- Chimney stacks: pointing, flaunching and whether they are capped.
- Valley gutters on older terraces, a classic leak point.

## 3. Drainage and services

- Drain survey if the property is older or has a shared drain.
- Water pressure and boiler age; replacement is a large single cost.
- Electrical consumer unit age and whether there is a recent test certificate.
- Gas safety record if a boiler or fire is present.

## 4. Construction-specific points

- **Victorian terraces**: solid walls, cellar damp, original slate, single-skin rear extensions.
- **Interwar semis**: cavity walls that may need ties checked, older wiring.
- **New-build apartments**: warranty terms, service charges, cladding and fire-safety documentation.
- **Converted mills**: sound insulation, service charges, maintenance obligations.

## 5. The financial questions a survey does not answer

Ask the seller or agent in writing:

1. When was the roof last worked on, and is there a guarantee?
2. Has the property had damp treatment, and is there a transferable guarantee?
3. What is the current service charge and what does it cover?
4. Are there any known disputes with neighbours or the freeholder?
5. What is included in the sale: fixtures, appliances, garden structures?

## 6. How to use the report

Do not treat every amber item as a reason to walk away. Convert findings into three numbers: immediate cost, cost within two years, and cost of routine upkeep. Then decide whether the price reflects them.

## 7. Checklist before you commit

1. Survey type appropriate to the age of the property.
2. Damp cause identified specifically.
3. Roof and gutters assessed with an age estimate.
4. Drainage checked where relevant.
5. Electrical and boiler documentation requested.
6. Questions to the seller answered in writing.
